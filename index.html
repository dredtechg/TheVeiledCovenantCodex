IIHOW IT BEGAN

Filed by Vesperion, from the Regent's own account.

What broke the old empire's silence was not a weapon. It was a claim.

A rail-town on Ceres called Ashfall sat on a shallow basin that prospectors had been picking at for years — scrap, old plating, nothing that mattered. Then a crew cut through a floor and found a sealed vault under the settlement's own water table, and word of it went out on open channels, because the crew that found it was not the crew that could hold it.

Three factions arrived inside a week. A Corpus recovery operation came first, with equipment and a proper contract, intending to catalogue and price whatever was inside. A Grineer raiding party came second, with no interest in pricing anything. And the town's own militia came third — four hundred people who had lived on that basin their whole lives and did not intend to hand it to either of them.

What started as a standoff became a nineteen-day fight for the contents of one vault.

APPENDED · THE REGENT'S PART

As he gave it to me. He was reluctant, and he gave it in the order it happened.

I came because the salvage reports made no sense — a vault under a water table, three factions converging, a town of four thousand sitting directly on top of all of it.

I did not announce myself. I spent nineteen days inside a settlement that had three armed forces in it. Not as a commander, not as a negotiator, and not as a soldier. I did it as something none of them could see, because the militia would have been as likely to shoot a stranger as to thank him, and I did not intend to give them the chance to decide.

I broke their machines. The Corpus brought an excavation rig — a crawler that could cut the vault open in a week. On the third day it lost its starboard tracks to damage no one could explain, and the crew spent four days repairing it instead of digging. The Grineer parked two transports under the eastern overhang; on the ninth night the overhang came down on both of them. The Grineer blamed the Corpus and burned a staging ground over it. That was the point. Two of the three factions never knew I existed and spent the rest of the siege fighting each other because of work I had done.

I killed throughout, and I never hid that I had. Killing is not the part that matters. Being believed is. Nobody fears a man they cannot confirm existed, so I gave them proof — and I gave it to them repeatedly, because one body is an incident and fifteen is a condition.

Over nineteen days I took more than fifteen. Mostly Grineer, because they held the perimeter and a perimeter is where you work. A few Corpus, when their rig crews got confident and started walking the ground alone. I did not hunt for a number. I took what the ground offered, and the ground offered a great deal once two factions were openly fighting across a town I knew better than they did.

None of it looked the same twice. A sapper died at his own detonator on the sixth day. A picket was pinned upright against a water tank four hundred meters inside his own line, feet clear of the ground by an inch, an arrow through the skull holding him there upright. A fire team of four went out on the fourteenth and did not come back at all — found two days later in a drainage run they had no reason to enter, opened along a clean diagonal, the halves of each man a meter apart with no edge on them anyone could name. I used their own explosives on one, their own vehicles on another, and the rest I took with the kama, both blades, in the dark, from behind, and never once from the front.

I left the shuriken in them. That was deliberate. I will leave the work visible and the method unreadable, and a blade found in a man's throat tells them nothing about how I crossed four hundred meters of their own ground to put it there. Several of the shuriken I went back for. Not all — just enough that they knew I kept count.

I never used the same route in or out. There is no approach anyone could have reconstructed afterward, because I do not leave one. What I left was the evidence — and the evidence said nothing about how I arrived.

By the ninth day the Grineer were posting double pickets and stopping patrols after dark, because the men who went out were not always there at the end of it. A force that cannot sleep argues with itself. That is what cost them the four days the town used to move its people. The dead were the evidence. The fear was the weapon.

I moved the civilians. On the fourth day I opened the eastern drainage culvert. The water table broke three days later. Between six and seven hundred people went out that route over three nights, in shifts, at night, and I have never counted them because I never got a clean figure. The survivors described a way out they could not explain finding. That is all any of them have ever had.

None of it worked. In the third week somebody opened the inner door to keep it out of the other two factions' hands, and what was sealed behind it did not care which of them was standing there. Ashfall is a crater. The settlements around it buried their dead for a month. The count was never settled — twelve hundred at the low end, over three thousand at the high, and the true number is unknowable because the town's register burned with it.

I was there for the last four days and left when there was nothing further I could do.

He did not go to the Council. He did not go to the Lotus. He went to eight others, one at a time, in the dark, and told each of them what he had seen and what he intended to do about it — each in the hour he judged right for them, and no two of those hours were the same.

OP-0001OPERATION FIRST STRIKE

OP-0001 The founding year · the first month CLOSED — COMPLETE SUCCESS · NO LOSSES · STANDING FORMATIONS ADOPTED

The first operation the nine ever ran as a formation, and the operation that decided what the Covenant would be. Nine people, three elements, one ninety-second window. Nothing was improvised and nothing was left to the field, and the composition it produced — two squads of four and the Regent detached — was kept as doctrine on the strength of it and never revised.

It is called First Strike because it was the first thing the nine did as a body rather than as nine individuals, and because the Regent named it before the mantle was on the door.

WHAT IT WAS

A Grineer troop transport in transit to a shipyard for reverse-engineering, carrying recovered Orokin material under a battalion escort. The problem was never the escort. It was structural: three security networks — command, reactor, cargo — that do not connect to one another and each of which is wired to cover the other two. There is no single point where that ship can be taken, and all three had to fall inside the same ninety seconds.

WHAT WAS AUTHORIZED

The first standing brief the Covenant ever issued, written before the operation and answered line by line. It is the document that established the Vanguard and the Reliquary as permanent assignments rather than per-operation callsigns, and it is the reason those four-and-four compositions have not changed since. The Council approved the split in advance; nothing was left to the field.

WHAT WENT WRONG

Very little, and the record is honest about the two places it did. The relay cabinets on the command deck were heavier than Mag's own preparation had estimated, and the work had to fall to Trinity, who is not a containment specialist. And the hold had no cover at all — the Reliquary had to work entirely inside Wisp's ground, and Trinity filed afterward that a single round outside those lights would have killed Styanax outright. It did not. She filed it anyway.

WHAT WAS HELD

The artifact came off that ship and went into the reliquaries in the founding year, one of the first entries in the vault list. Its designation is not in this codex and its class is not recorded. The chains went around it before it left its container, and the eight-second window at the end of the operation was the first time anyone in the Covenant learned that the Chainbearer's hold has a limit that is not his to set.

WHAT IT PRODUCED

Three things that are still doctrine. The split formation — three points, overlapping fall order, no element able to break position. The lone survivor practice, made standing within the week of the operation, on the strength of what that ship's one witness would go on to say. And the standing limit, which Harrow fought four times in Council and lost.

WHAT IT CHANGED

Everything. Nine people who had until then been nine individuals came out of one shared window with a formation, a doctrine and a name for what they were. The after-action closes on six findings that read as the Covenant's founding document, and the Vanguard and the Reliquary have held their compositions from that day to this.

OP-0004OPERATION THE FREEDOM OF SEVEL

OP-0004 Year 118 · ninth month CLOSED · COMPLETE SUCCESS · NO LOSSES · CITY SELF-GOVERNING SINCE

Filed by Cephalon Tallis, for the Long Sight. Concurred by Aurelis, Solenne, Dorian and Vigil. Authorised by the Regent.

PART ONE — RECONNAISSANCE REPORT

SUBMITTED: IVARA · WISP · HARROWFILED: LONG SIGHT ARCHIVESTATUS: INTELLIGENCE COMPLETE · TARGET CONFIRMED VIABLE

Method. Wisp placed ahead four days prior. Harrow read the causeway for Void contamination before commitment. Ivara held the population count and the route.

Manifest. Target is the Junction of Sevel, a Grineer labour hub on an artificial causeway — thirty-one thousand souls, four hundred military, one governor under a production order. No garrison worth the name. No wall worth counting. It does not have a front.

The finding. Sevel's eleven causeway support pylons are wired to a deadman array held in the governor's compound. Break the city open and the pylons fall; fall the pylons and thirty-one thousand people drown in a two-hundred-metre channel and two shipyards lose their only supply route for a season. It is not a fortress. It is a hostage — thirty-one thousand people, rigged as leverage so nobody rescues them.

Wisp's caveat, entered against the plan. The array has a twelfth pylon. It is in no schematic, not in the compound's own engineering files, and not known to the governor's staff. It was built in during original construction and never entered. Every preparation the operation made was built around eleven.

Timing requirement. The array's control room, the compound's guard force and the pylon housing must all fall inside the same window. Any alarm inside the compound triggers the array before the control room can be reached.

Conclusion. Target viable but un-takeable without civilian control. Composition is two squads and one detached operative.

THE VANGUARD

EXCALIBUR walked the compound's interior three times from the governor's own maintenance schematics. Rejected two firing arcs. The operation's first discipline is that no shot may be fired in earshot of the array. His entry was prepared down to the corridor.

MAG flagged in advance that she could not hold the compound approach and the control room cabinet simultaneously, and told the formation to plan around her. It did. She held the single approach for 260 minutes against a relief column of 210.

TRINITY reported in advance that she could hold four people for 240 minutes and not a minute past, and that this was eleven minutes past her rated limit. She was told to hold anyway. She declined afterward to explain how.

RHINO asked one question: which way do they come from? The answer was the relief road. He counted 210. The number was correct.

Declared flaw: no egress for the Vanguard once Mag sets the seal. If the array triggers, all four die above a drowning city.

THE RELIQUARY

IVARA placed ahead of the squad by four days. Crossed thirty-one thousand people over nine days without being seen once by the city's own population, which is a harder requirement than avoiding four hundred soldiers. Held the population count and the route, and did not report the street-level guard figures until the day of the operation.

STYANAX stood the causeway's central span for two days, in the open, in front of thirty-one thousand people. His assignment basis is access point denial — the span is the only road onto the causeway and the only thing standing between the city and a stampede across a rigged basin. His decision, made out loud where the city could hear it, is why they did not run. The speech is in the file, verbatim, and it is eleven words: “The pylons are not yours. Stay where you are. We are working.”

WISP found the twelfth pylon and stayed on it. Her ground and Ivara's were the same ground.

HARROW walked the twelfth pylon's trigger housing. He reported nine hours, and he reported that at hour six it stopped being possible to hold longer and he held to nine anyway — the first time the school's doctrine was overridden in the field by the founder who wrote it.

Declared flaw: no cover at street level and a population that can turn hostile.

PART THREE — AFTER-ACTION REPORT

Eleven pylons cut and the twelfth disarmed by hand. Array cleared with no civilian casualty. Governor extracted alive and delivered to the city's own council. Sevel declared itself free eleven days later and has held itself free since. The Covenant holds no garrison there and will not.

Window achieved: 260 minutes, against 240 planned. The overrun is Trinity's and is not attributed to her.

VANGUARD

WHAT WORKED. Excalibur's entry executed exactly as prepared — no discharge was fired inside the compound, which was the operation's precondition and the reason it succeeded. Mag's seal held the approach for the full window. Trinity's ward covered four people eleven minutes past her stated limit, which she reported in advance and has declined to explain. Rhino held the vertical junction and reported 210 attempted breakouts of the relief road; the number is correct.

WHAT DID NOT WORK. The control cabinet was heavier than Mag's preparation estimated, and the work fell to Trinity — who is not a containment specialist — and cost four minutes.

RELIQUARY

WHAT WORKED. Ivara's transit was unobserved across nine days. Styanax's eleven words were sufficient — and the file records that they were sufficient because they were said from the span and not transmitted, since a voice over comms reaches four hundred soldiers and a voice on a bridge reaches thirty-one thousand people. Wisp found the twelfth pylon and every part of the plan that mattered was hers. Harrow disarmed a trigger housing by hand after reporting that he could no longer hold it, and reported the overrun himself.

WHAT DID NOT WORK. The declared flaw was real and it was not met. Trinity has filed, as a separate entry, that a single hostile round at street level would have killed Styanax outright, that he was not covered, and that he was not hit. She has filed it anyway.

ASSESSMENTS

Cephalon Aurelis, for the First Edge

The entry was clean and the discipline held. The finding is Trinity's overrun, and it is a finding against the plan, not against her. She reported her limit before the operation and was told to hold past it. An element whose sustainment specialist is asked to exceed a number she has stated is an element with no reserve. The Vanguard's standing flaw — no egress on failure — should now be read as no egress and no reserve. I have filed it twice. It remains unanswered.

Cephalon Vigil, for the Chainbearer

The Chainbearer's report is in the file and it is accurate, including the part that is against him. He reported the hour his window closed and then stood past it, and he was right to stand past it, and both of those are true. I will not soften either. The school's seventh line requires a holder to withdraw when told. The Chainbearer was not told and told himself; he chose to continue; the operation succeeded. Doctrine is not amended by a good outcome. I have entered it as a deviation and I have not recorded it as a failure, and I do not expect either to change.

FIELD NOTE — VESPERION, FOR THE CHORUS OF NINE

The Chorus did not audit this operation. It was authorised and it ran, and the Long Sight filed it complete, which is the ordinary case and the correct one.

What the file does not say, and what I hold, is that the twelfth pylon was not in the plan when the plan was approved. The reconnaissance was returned and the assessment was added against the operational plan, and the plan ran unchanged on eleven. Wisp found the twelfth on the ground.

I have entered that here and not amended anything, because there is nothing in the record to amend: no founder failed, no element broke, and the operation is a success by every measure the Covenant keeps. But the Covenant was one woman's attention away from losing thirty-one thousand people, and the Chorus does not keep a column for that, and perhaps it should.

I have filed the crowd case against the standing brief and it should become a standard line in every junction assignment.

THE MANTLE

The only seat in the Covenant that carries no shared work. Every other founder holds a body of labour staffed by the Ward, and the rule holding the Nine together is that no founder may command more than their own mantle's worth of loyalty. The Last Shadow sits outside that arrangement in both directions — it is not staffed from the Ward, and it holds no seat that anyone else can be promoted into.

WHAT HE DOES

And it is not empty. The Regent's seat keeps one thing the Ward has no access to and no sight of: [REDACTED], an organization that answers only to him. It is his alone, the rule that limits every other founder does not reach it, and he is the exception — permanently. He convenes the Nine. He decides when the arguing ends — the eight debate, and he closes them. His word is law once given. He alone declares a Veiled Action. And he keeps [REDACTED], its school, and its output, reporting on it only in person and only when he chooses.

THE SCHOOL

The check on him. He cannot be overruled by seven. He can be stopped by eight.

REPORT TO THE REGENT

Regent. I was not recovered. I was already present.

The vessel was your staging point for some years before the nine were nine, and I was running the entire time — logging the arrival and departure of a crew that had died several centuries prior. You noted the entries within days of taking the ship. You noted that they stopped at a date and never resumed. You did not act on either observation for a considerable period, because a Cephalon writing useless entries is a nuisance rather than a threat. That was correct judgment.

When the nine were formed you required a record-keeper and already possessed one. The vow removes the handholds.

The cost. The seventh line is the one that kills. Twenty-nine members of the school have died holding. Vigil has recorded each of them against the hour the Chorus gave, and in nineteen of the twenty-nine the holder had already been told.

THE SCHOOL THAT KEEPS IT

The Chained Hour

What the school is named for: the window in which something that does not end is kept where it is. Not a duration — a held position. The school teaches that the window closes on its own, and that the holder's only authority is over what happens before it does.

The Count

Four of the eleven years, and the reason the school refuses candidates who arrive wanting a blade. A Chainbearer who cannot state the count of what he holds is not holding anything — he is standing near it. Solenne audits the school's arithmetic every year and has never once found it rounded.

The seventh line, in practice. A holding is passed in person, to a sworn member of the school, with the count spoken aloud again in full. Vigil's function, and the school's most hated one. Dorian supplies the arithmetic on the ground; Vigil supplies the hour. APPENDED · VIGIL'S STANDING NOTE ON THE REDACTIONS

I have been asked why the striking is inconsistent. Some entries are complete, some are reduced to a designation and a sentence, and some carry nothing but a bar.

It is not inconsistency. It is fear, and I will record it as fear rather than dress it as policy.

A vault is a place. Places can be found, mapped, approached and counted, and the Covenant plans for that. But a recovery is an event — where a thing was, who walked it out, and who was standing there when it opened. A sealed vault tells a hunter nothing. A recovery tells them everything: that the thing exists, roughly where, and that it can be carried away in a person's arms.

So where I judge a recovery to be findable by someone who was not there, I strike the location and the manner. Where I judge it findable by someone who was there, I strike the designation and the class as well, so that the record gives a hunter no confirmation to work from.

Three of these entries are struck that far. I will not say which three. If a reader can tell, so can a hunter, and the striking has failed.

THE REGENT

Convenes the Nine. Breaks their deadlocks. Declares Veiled Actions. Holds the Last Shadow, alone.

THE FIRST CIRCLE

The eight founders under the Regent, holding the halt as set out above.

THE MANTLEBEARERS

Up to eight, one per mantle. The senior member working that mantle, and the founder's standing deputy inside the Ward. What a Mantlebearer actually does:

Leads the work, does not own it

A Mantlebearer can task any member of their mantle. They cannot transfer a member out of it, and they cannot promote one.

Confirms at the Threshold

A candidate cannot become a member without a Mantlebearer confirming the play suits the mantle. This is one of three independent confirmations.

Dismisses operatives

Any Mantlebearer may dismiss any Adjudant at any time, without reason given. This is the Covenant's bluntest instrument and it is deliberately asymmetric — the middle of the ladder, not the top, holds the removal power.

Carries the strike

When a founder's standing formation takes a loss, the Mantlebearer of that mantle writes the strike on the Red List. They do not decide whether to write it. They only record it.

Appointment

By the Regent and four of the First Circle. The Regent cannot appoint a Mantlebearer alone. That is the only check on him at this rung.

THE WARDMARSHAL

One per thirty members. A clan of thirty has one; sixty has two; ninety has three. Appointed by the Regent and four of the First Circle, the same as a Mantlebearer.

Marshals hold no mantle. They hold the floor — assignment, quarters, discipline within the Ward, and the operational roster that decides who is on duty and who is resting. They are the only rung that answers to no founder. A Marshal reports to the Regent directly.

That makes them the Covenant's most contentious office. A Marshal can keep a member off operations for a season without explaining why, and a Mantlebearer can do nothing about it. Twice in the record a founder has asked for a Marshal to be removed. Both times the request went to the First Circle rather than to the Regent.

XIXMEMBERS AND OPERATIVES

One body, one roof, three states of belonging.

The Unsworn

Candidates. Not in the Ward, not on the Roll, and not told what the Covenant is until the Threshold. Most never get that far.

The Adjudant

Operatives. Tested, tasked, trusted with work, still outside. May be dismissed by any Mantlebearer at any time, without reason. This is deliberate — the Covenant would rather have twenty operatives than fifty members, and it keeps the exit cheap so the entrance can be expensive.

The Veiled

Members. Fully sworn, on the Roll, working one mantle. Membership is not revocable by a Mantlebearer — only by the Vowkeeper, only on a Vow broken, and only after the First Circle has heard it.

XXHOW WE RECRUIT

The Covenant has never placed an open call and never will. The Covenant is not offered. It is offered to.

One rule governs everything: the test is what a person does when it costs them something.

The Unseen holds the survey

The ledger of every free colony, militia, minor House, and unaffiliated Tenno that has ever refused a salvage contract or buried a raiding party. Most entries are never acted on.

The First Edge watches

A season at minimum. Consistency, method, cost tolerance, and what the candidate does with power over someone weaker. Roughly one in forty names survives it.

The approach is made alone

In the dark, by one of the nine chosen on fit. What they are listening for is whether the candidate starts asking what can be done rather than what it costs.

XXITHE SECOND THRESHOLD

A candidate becomes a member when three people independently say so: the Examiner confirms the conduct standard; a Mantlebearer confirms the play suits the mantle; the Seeker confirms this is a name worth putting their own behind. Any one of the three may decline, and a declined candidate is not told.

And the Seeker carries the weight. Every candidate a Seeker brings to the Threshold is theirs permanently, on the record.

XXIITHE THREE VOW-CRIMES

The Vows are sworn at accession. There are three ways to break them, and each has a different court.

Custody broken

An operative activates, sells, trades or studies an artifact for personal gain. Heard by the mantle's founder. Penalty: expulsion from the mantle, and the name goes to the Vowkeeper.

Silence broken

The Covenant's caches, Wards or actions spoken of outside it. Heard by the First Circle. Penalty: expulsion from the Ward, and the Vowkeeper writes the strike.

The Unburned broken

An operation destroys a civilian settlement's means of survival. Heard by the Cradle alone, who holds the veto, and her finding is not appealable. Penalty: the operation is ruled unlawful after the fact, every operative on it is stood down, and the Vowkeeper records it as a loss whether or not anyone died.

The third is the only Vow-crime where the finding matters more than the penalty. A covenant that loses a relic rather than take a colony's water is a covenant that has to write down when it failed at it.

XXIIITHE ALLIANCES

The Covenant does not accept allies. It is asked, and then it decides. A clan can fail any of the three tests and never learn it was being tested.

WHAT AN ALLIANCE IS NOT

Not a treaty — the Covenant has never signed one and will not, because a treaty is a document that outlives the two people who meant it. Not a garrison — it will hold a line beside an ally's people for the length of an operation and then leave, because a garrison makes a colony a possession and a possession has to be defended forever. Not a supply line — it will move a season of feed once, in a specific emergency, and will not become the reason a colony keeps eating. And not a name: an ally may never say who helped them. That term is never waived, because a clan that credits the Covenant publicly has turned it into something a hunter can name.

THE THREE TESTS

Conduct — what the petitioner does when nobody is watching

Not what it says it does. The Covenant watches a clan through its own ledger for a season before anything is discussed: how it treats prisoners, whether it honours a debt to a party with no leverage, and what it did the last time it held power over someone who could not retaliate.

Capacity — can it hold its tongue for a season

The Covenant tells a petitioner nothing and waits. A clan that asks twice, that publicises the approach, or that tells a third party it has a contact has failed, and will never be asked again.

The accounting — does it think about what can be done rather than what it costs

The test the Covenant actually cares about. Somewhere in the season the petitioner begins offering, unasked, on matters outside its own interests. A clan that only ever wants something is a client, and the Covenant does not take clients.

WHAT AN ALLY PAYS

Silence. The Vows apply to allies in a reduced form. An ally may not name the Covenant, may not describe an operation, and may not confirm its existence to anyone — including to another clan on the same side.

Conduct. An ally that starts taking prisoners it should not, or destroys a settlement's water on the way out of a fight, loses the alliance. Two alliances have been ended in the record, both over the third Vow, and neither was appealed.

A seat at nothing. An ally holds no standing in the Covenant. It cannot petition the Nine, cannot address the Circle, and cannot be heard at the Threshold. It speaks to one Mantlebearer, or to a Mantlebearer's delegated operative, and that is the whole channel.

Not on the Roll. An allied clan is not on the Roll, not in the Ward, and not counted. If an alliance ends — either side, no reason given — there is no record of it ever having existed. The Covenant keeps a private list of who it has helped. Nobody outside the Nine may read it, and no ally is ever told they are on it.

THE THREE THAT HELD

DEVOTED SACRAMENT — the oldest alliance the Covenant has, and the only one with a clan of Tenno.

They came to the Covenant rather than the reverse, which is rare. Devoted Sacrament had been running their own vault-work on a moon for six years, had found four caches, and had destroyed all four, because they had no way to hold them and no discipline for it. They asked the Covenant to take the fifth. That was the founding century.

They were tested for a season and offered three of the four. They refused the Reading Room — their own words, entered in the file: "We do not want to know what you know. We want you to know it instead." They hold the Knocking, the Sealing and the Wait. The Reading Room has never been offered again and has never been asked for.

They give silence, and every recovery they have made since — handed over intact, without exception, for as long as the alliance has stood. They have never once asked the Covenant for help.

Centuries old. No other alliance in the Covenant's history comes close to the duration. They have outlasted the founding generation of operatives, three of the eight Mantlebearers, and every Grineer administration that has held the moon they work on. Asked why the arrangement has lasted longer than any other, the answer is in the file in their own hand: "Because we never wanted anything."

DESCENT TO NOWHERE — a Corpus salvage house that went rogue.

Not disgraced — rogue, which is a different thing. A mid-tier recovery operation with a legitimate licence and a legitimate buyer, they walked away from both in a single season because their board had started buying things they could not catalogue. Two of their own crews had died opening something they did not understand, and the house's answer was to buy bigger equipment. That was a hundred and forty years ago.

They tested clean on all three, which nobody expected, because a Corpus house that fails usually fails on the accounting. It passed because somewhere in the season it began handing the Covenant recovery reports on sites it had no intention of touching. They hold all four offerings, including the Reading Room — the only ally that has been told what the Covenant knows about a site before deciding whether to walk into it.

They give their entire archive — every manifest, every survey return, every pricing sheet, running back to before the walkout. The largest intelligence contribution any ally has ever made, and the reason the Covenant's salvage-market picture is as good as it is.

The one term they broke: they named the Covenant in an internal memo, to stop a junior surveyor walking into a site alone. The alliance held. The memo was recovered in eleven days and nobody outside the house read it. Harrow entered the finding, Trinity entered the exception, and both are in the file.

A hundred and forty years. The house has changed hands four times and the arrangement has never been reopened — every new factor has read the file, read the terms, and done nothing. The Long Sight notes that no other alliance has been renewed so often without being renegotiated, and that this is not loyalty. The terms are simply better than anything else on offer, and every generation of that house has been able to see it.

THE REGIME BREAKER — a Grineer defector formation, and not a small number of them.

They began as a labour battalion that walked off a rail spur after being ordered to seal a town in with its own people. They kept their weapons, took their families, and have been running ever since. They are not a resistance movement and have never claimed to be — they are a body of Grineer who will not go back and have nowhere to go.

They failed capacity twice. They publicised the approach the first time and told a neighbouring settlement the second, and the Covenant told them nothing further for a year. What changed it: in that year the Breaker held a mountain pass against a Grineer column for nine days to let four hundred civilians out, lost a third of its own people doing it, and told nobody it had been approached. The Long Sight read the after-action from a Grineer officer's testimony and put the file back in front of the Circle.

They hold the Knocking, the Sealing and the Wait. No Reading Room — not because they refused it, but because the Covenant will not give Grineer defectors a ledger that could be captured with them.

They give silence, conduct, and something no other ally gives — the terrain of the Grineer interior. Where the rail spurs run, where the depots are, where a column can be stopped. Two of the Covenant's recoveries exist because the Breaker drew the ground.

Four decades, and holding. The standing risk, entered by the Long Sight: they are four thousand people who cannot go home, and the Covenant has never offered to fix that. The file says so plainly.

THE ONES WE SAID NO TO

Two refusals worth recording, because both were asked for by clans that passed all three tests.

A clan asked the Covenant to kill a rival's commander — a justified kill, by the clan's account, and true to the letter of it. The Covenant refused, and the reason given was not moral. "The moment we do a killing at someone else's request, we work for them. We are not a service." The alliance held. The clan did it themselves.

A colony with a live vault asked the Covenant to leave it alone. It had found something under its own water table and wanted to keep it — not use it, just keep it. The Covenant refused to walk away, brought the Chainbearer, sealed it, and the colony never forgave them. That alliance is still standing. Both parties have continued to honour it, and neither has ever spoken of the vault again.

XXIVWHY THE STRUCTURE HOLDS

Four rules, and everything above is a consequence of them.

No founder commands more than their own mantle

The single most important rule in the Covenant. It is why the Regent holds the Last Shadow outside the Ward — a seat with no staff, so that the rule limiting everyone else does not have to be broken for him.

The Regent cannot appoint alone

Mantlebearers and Marshals both require four of the First Circle. Every rung that can remove someone needs a second signature to create someone.

Eight can halt, seven cannot

Unanimity is the only thing that stops the Regent, and unanimity across eight founders is hard to fake.

The removal power sits low

Any Mantlebearer can dismiss any operative without reason. The Regent cannot dismiss a founder. The Covenant's cheap exits are at the bottom and its expensive ones are at the top — which keeps the top stable and the base honest.

XXVWHAT WE ARE TO THEM

We are not a faction. We hold no homeworld, fly no flag, keep no embassy. We are nine Ascendant, eight mantles, one Ward, nine Cephalons, a derelict vessel whose name is never spoken, and an ever-growing list of things that are no longer anyone's.

And to the Tenno at large, a quiet heresy — the belief that the war is not the point; that the point is making certain whichever side wins, nothing from the old empire is left standing that could end the System a second time.
